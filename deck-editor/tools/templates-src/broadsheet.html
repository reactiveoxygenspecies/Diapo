Exclusive · The year in review

Small team doubles output, keeps its standards

Twelve months, three decisions and one surprising result, reported from the inside

By Your Name, Staff Writer

Every year begins with a question nobody can yet answer. This one asked whether a team of twelve could hold its standards while doubling what it shipped. The answer, twelve months on, is mostly yes.

The exceptions are the interesting part: two projects that slipped, one cancelled, and the quiet autumn week that changed the plan.

Three decisions that made the difference

The first decision was to plan in seasons rather than quarters. Each season carried one theme, and every project had to earn its place under that theme or wait for the next.

It sounds like a change of vocabulary. It was a change of behaviour: projects that did not fit were no longer squeezed in.

The second was slower and harder. The team stopped measuring effort and started measuring what shipped. Meetings shrank. Documents grew. The work improved.

"We stopped being busy," one engineer said, "and started being finished."

The third decision was made in a single week in October, when the team paused new work entirely and repaired what it had. That week is the subject of the interview on page 6.

What follows is the record: the projects, the numbers, and the voices of the people who did the work.
