Welcome
to Diapo

Everything here is editable: try it as you read. Press → or Space to go on.

These are speaker notes. They show in the presenter view (P) and in the panel on the right when the slide is selected.
01 · Select

Click it,
drag it

  • Click anything on the slide to select it; the panel on the right shows what it is
  • Drag to move; the handles resize (Shift keeps the proportions, Alt grows from the centre); the handle above the box rotates
  • Shift+click adds to the selection; Ctrl+click picks the exact element under the cursor; Alt+drag duplicates it
  • Arrow keys nudge by a pixel, Shift+arrows by ten; smart guides snap to edges and centres

02 · Edit text

Double-click
any text

It edits in place. Enter makes a new line, Esc finishes. While editing, Ctrl+B, Ctrl+I and Ctrl+U format the selection, and the small bar above the text has links, lists and alignment.

“Change a word in me.”

A diapositive is a positive you look through. So is a good deck: the light comes from behind.

03 · Add

Slides and
what goes on them

  • The + above the slide strip (or Ctrl+M) opens the layout picker: title, bullets, two columns, image and caption, quote, big number, and more
  • Insert in the top bar adds text, lists, tables, code, pictures, video, shapes and icons to this slide
  • Image placeholders from a layout are filled by double-clicking them

04 · Arrange

Copy, group,
stack

  • Ctrl+C, Ctrl+X and Ctrl+V work on elements, also across slides and between decks; pictures and text from other programs paste too
  • Ctrl+G groups the selection, Ctrl+Shift+G ungroups
  • Ctrl+] and Ctrl+[ move an element one step forward or back in the stacking order
  • Right-click an element for all of this in a menu

05 · Style

One element,
or the whole deck

The Colour, Text and Box sections of the panel change the selected element. Swatches come from the deck's own theme colours, and fonts from the deck first, then Google Fonts.

Theme (Ctrl+T) changes the whole deck: its colour variables, its base text styles and free CSS, all written into one block the deck can live without.
