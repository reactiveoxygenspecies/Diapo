Concept

The inverted pyramid

Put the conclusion first, the reasons second, and the background last. A reader who stops after one sentence still has the point.

Most drafts do the reverse: they build up to the point, and the reader leaves before it.

Exercise 1
10minutes

Rewrite this headline so it says what happened

  1. Take the headline on your handout: "Update on the Q3 planning process".
  2. Write three versions. Each must contain a verb and a fact.
  3. Swap with your neighbour and pick their best one.

Space below for drawing during the exercise (press D).

Start the timer on the wall clock. Walk the room; collect two good rewrites to show.
Discussion
When did you last stop reading something after one line? Why?
What was the first sentence? What did you expect instead? Would a headline have saved it?
Exercise 2 · In threes

Cut the memo in half

1

Reader

Reads the memo once, out loud, and says where they got lost.

2

Cutter

Strikes every sentence the reader did not need. Aim for half.

3

Judge

Checks that nothing important went; restores at most one sentence.

Quick check

Which opening line follows the inverted pyramid?

AAs you may know, our planning process has evolved over the years.
BWe are moving Q3 planning to a single two-week window in June.
CThis memo covers several topics related to planning.
DPlanning is important to all of us.
B: it states the decision first; everything else can follow.
Recap

Four things to take back to your desk

1

The headline says what happened, with a verb.

2

The first sentence is the conclusion.

3

Every paragraph can lose a sentence. Usually the first.

4

Read it aloud once before sending.
